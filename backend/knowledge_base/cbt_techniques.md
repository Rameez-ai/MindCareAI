# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) Techniques

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is a structured, action-oriented form of psychotherapy. It is based on the premise that our thoughts (cognitions), feelings (emotions), and behaviors (actions) are interconnected, and that identifying and changing negative thought patterns can lead to changes in feelings and behaviors.

## 1. Identifying Cognitive Distortions
Cognitive distortions are biased ways of thinking that are common when someone is struggling with their mood. Recognising these distortions is the first step in challenging them.

- **All-or-Nothing Thinking (Black-and-White Thinking)**: Seeing things in binary categories. If a situation is not perfect, it is seen as a total failure. (e.g., "If I don't get an A on this exam, I'm a complete failure.")
- **Catastrophizing**: Anticipating the worst possible outcome, even when it is highly unlikely. (e.g., "If I stumble during my presentation, everyone will laugh at me, and I'll get fired.")
- **Mind Reading**: Assuming you know what others are thinking, usually believing they think negatively of you, without any proof. (e.g., "She didn't wave back, she must hate me.")
- **Overgeneralization**: Taking a single negative event and viewing it as a never-ending pattern of defeat. (e.g., "I messed up this interview. I will never get a job.")
- **Emotional Reasoning**: Believing that because you feel a certain way, it must be reality. (e.g., "I feel so overwhelmed, my life must be a mess.")

## 2. The Thought Record (Three-Column Technique)
A classic CBT tool to challenge negative thoughts.
1. **Situation**: Describe the trigger. (Who, what, where?)
2. **Automatic Thought**: Identify the underlying negative statement you told yourself. Measure your belief in this thought (0-100%).
3. **Alternative Thought (Rational Response)**: Challenge the automatic thought by looking at the objective evidence. Find a balanced, alternative perspective.

## 3. Behavioral Activation
Behavioral activation involves deliberately planning activities that bring a sense of pleasure or achievement to counteract low motivation and low mood.
- Create a list of activities (small and large).
- Schedule them in a planner.
- Commit to performing them even if you don't "feel" like it.
- Reflect on your mood before and after the activity.
