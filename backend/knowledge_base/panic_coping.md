# Coping with Panic Attacks

A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear or discomfort that reaches a peak within minutes. Though highly terrifying, it is not physically dangerous and will eventually pass.

## 1. Grounding in the Midst of Panic
- **Acknowledge and Accept**: Tell yourself: "I am having a panic attack. It is uncomfortable, but I am safe, and it will pass." Fighting the panic can worsen the feedback loop.
- **Focus on the Breath**: Hyperventilation is common. Take slow, shallow breaths: inhale for 3 seconds, hold for 1 second, exhale for 3 seconds.
- **Find an Anchor**: Focus entirely on a physical object in your environment (e.g., watch the secondhand on a clock, touch the texture of a chair, squeeze an ice cube).

## 2. Cold Water Reset (Mammalian Dive Reflex)
- If panic starts rising, splash ice-cold water on your face or hold an ice pack to your chest.
- This triggers a physiological response that lowers your heart rate and redirects blood flow, helping slow down the panic response.

## 3. Cognitive Re-framing for Panic
- "These symptoms are caused by adrenaline. They are not a sign that I am having a heart attack or going crazy."
- "My body is doing its job of trying to protect me, it's just overreacting right now. I will let the adrenaline wash through me and subside."
